## Business Question

How do attrition rates vary by work environment satisfaction, job satisfaction, relationship satisfaction, work-life balance, and overtime among employees with less than five years of tenure?　



## Key Findings — Employees with Less Than 5 Years of Tenure

**1. Overtime is strongly associated with higher attrition rates.**

- Employees who work overtime have an attrition rate of 43.10%, compared with 16.12% among those who do not work overtime.

**2. Lower satisfaction ratings are generally associated with higher attrition rates.**

- Employees reporting lower job satisfaction, environment satisfaction, and relationship satisfaction generally show higher attrition rates. However, the relationship is not consistently linear across all satisfaction levels.

**3. Work-life balance shows a non-linear relationship with attrition.**

- Employees with the lowest work-life balance rating have the highest attrition rate (40.00%). However, employees with the highest rating (29.63%) have a higher attrition rate than those with a rating of 3 (21.29%).

- These results suggest that work-life balance may be associated with attrition, but higher ratings do not consistently correspond to lower attrition rates.



## Recommendations

- Investigate overtime patterns. Examine workload, overtime frequency, and potential differences across job roles to better understand the observed association with attrition.
   
- Prioritize employee satisfaction assessments. Review feedback from employees with lower job and environment satisfaction ratings to identify potential areas for improvement.

- Investigate work-life balance differences. Review group sizes and additional employee characteristics before drawing conclusions about the non-linear pattern.



## Limitations

- The analysis is descriptive and does not establish causal relationships.
- Group sizes should be reviewed before interpreting differences in attrition rates.
- The dataset is fictional and may not represent actual workforce behavior.
